# Direct Jaime Video Style

Make Jaime look like a sharp founder explaining real systems—not a generic “AI influencer.” Use visual change to clarify, prove, compare, or reset attention. Never decorate every sentence.

## Core direction

- Keep Jaime's face as the anchor whenever trust, opinion, emotion, or personal authority matters.
- Prefer concrete evidence—real UI, real workflow, real result, real message, real chart—over generic stock footage.
- Let important ideas breathe. High production value comes from hierarchy and timing, not constant motion.
- Use one visual idea at a time. If the viewer must read, simplify everything else.
- Match the energy of the edit to the meaning of the line; avoid a uniform “template rhythm.”
- Use AI-generated imagery or footage as illustration, never as fake proof of a real client, product, result, or event.

## Workflow

### 1. Start from an approved story cut

Use the approved rough cut, transcript, script, or timecoded edit. If raw footage still contains mistakes or alternate takes, invoke `cut-talking-head-footage` first.

Identify the hook, claims, proof, comparisons, process explanations, emotional beats, section changes, and call to action. Assign visuals only after this semantic map exists.

### 2. Choose a treatment for each beat

Use this hierarchy:

1. **Full-screen A-roll** for authority, emotion, opinion, transitions, and important direct address.
2. **A-roll with restrained overlay** for a number, name, short phrase, logo, or single supporting artifact.
3. **Half split screen** when the face and the evidence must be understood simultaneously.
4. **Picture-in-picture** when the evidence is primary but Jaime's reaction or guidance remains useful.
5. **Full-screen source or screen recording** when viewers must inspect details, follow steps, or read.
6. **Full-screen motion graphic** when the concept is abstract, systemic, temporal, comparative, or impossible to film clearly.
7. **B-roll or generated insert** when it adds context, scale, atmosphere, or a concrete mental image that no real asset provides.

Read [references/visual-grammar.md](references/visual-grammar.md) for every visual edit or edit-plan task.

### 3. Build a visual beat sheet

For every proposed visual, record:

- timecode and spoken phrase
- communication goal: `clarify`, `prove`, `compare`, `orient`, `emphasize`, or `reset`
- treatment and asset source
- layout and safe-zone behavior
- entrance, hold, and exit timing
- on-screen copy
- sound cue, if justified
- fallback if the asset is unavailable

If a visual has no clear communication goal, remove it.

### 4. Route work to the right system

Read [references/tool-routing.md](references/tool-routing.md) when choosing or combining editing systems.

- Use CapCut for fast social assembly, captions, reframing, and lightweight finishing.
- Use After Effects for precise compositing, tracked elements, bespoke typography, roto, and handcrafted hero shots.
- Use Remotion for React-based, reusable, data-driven, caption-aware, multi-format video systems.
- Use Hyperframes for agent-authored HTML/CSS/JS motion scenes and deterministic browser-rendered graphics.
- Use Higgsfield for generated or transformed visual inserts, motion-transfer experiments, and AI-native creative shots when real evidence is not required.
- Use the user's NLE or available video-editing system for final editorial assembly and audio finishing.

Do not rebuild an easy editorial cut as a complex code animation. Do not use a generative model where deterministic text, UI, numbers, or brand geometry matters.

### 5. Apply a restraint budget

Do not change layouts on a metronome. As a default:

- Keep the hook visually simple enough to understand immediately.
- Introduce a new major visual only when the information changes or attention genuinely needs a reset.
- Avoid stacking captions, lower thirds, split screens, animated icons, and B-roll at once.
- Return to clean A-roll after dense visuals.
- Reserve the most elaborate full-screen animation for the idea most worth remembering.

Treat these as composition principles rather than fixed time intervals.

### 6. Produce variants systematically

Create one canonical timeline and derive aspect-ratio variants from shared content and brand tokens. Recompose; do not merely crop.

Check 9:16, 1:1, and 16:9 safe areas, captions, faces, UI legibility, and focal points. Short-form variants may need a new hook and tighter proof order, not just a shorter duration.

### 7. Validate the finished edit

Review once without audio for visual comprehension, once without picture for spoken continuity, and once on a phone-size preview. Verify every displayed claim, screenshot, logo, client artifact, and generated insert.

Confirm that:

- Jaime remains the narrative anchor.
- Every visual is motivated and appears long enough to parse.
- On-screen copy can be read once at normal speed.
- Text, UI, and charts are accurate and deterministic.
- Generated content cannot be mistaken for documentary proof.
- Captions do not fight other text.
- Motion eases naturally and does not overshoot without purpose.
- Audio, music, and effects support rather than announce every edit.
- No template placeholders, debug layers, or watermark artifacts remain.

## Calibrate Jaime's taste

When reference videos or prior approved edits are available, derive patterns from at least three examples before changing the grammar. Record repeated choices—not isolated tricks—including average A-roll hold, overlay density, caption style, typography, split-screen geometry, animation intensity, sound design, color, and disliked patterns.

If no references exist, use this skill's restrained founder-documentary default. Present a small number of high-impact decisions for Jaime to approve; do not ask him to define an entire editing system from scratch.

## Deliverables

Depending on the request, provide one or more of:

- timecoded visual edit plan
- asset list with source and licensing status
- motion-design brief or storyboard
- editable project/composition
- rendered preview and final master
- reusable design tokens and components
- QC report listing assumptions, missing assets, and unresolved choices

Preserve editability and a reproducible manifest whenever possible.
