# Jaime's B-Roll Visual Identity ("our own style")

_This is **our** visual DNA — the locked look, soul, and signature moves for Jaime's
reels. It's the visual twin of `voice-dna.md`: `voice-dna` owns how Jaime **writes**,
this owns how his reels **look and move**. Read it **first** when directing b-roll —
before `Skills/reel-broll-director/references/broll-style.md` — so every reel comes out
recognizably his, not a different creator each time._

## 0. What this is (and isn't)

- **It is** an opinionated identity: ONE concept world, a fixed palette, a small set of
  owned devices. The point is **coherence** — a stranger should be able to tell two of
  Jaime's reels were made by the same person.
- **It is built for our real production:** Jaime films a **talking-head, storytelling-led
  reel** (Mexican Spanish, ~3 min) and the b-roll is **AI-generated** (Omni Flash et al.)
  to *explain and dynamize* what he's saying. Every choice here is therefore
  **AI-generatable** — real objects, real light, real motion (which generate well),
  never handmade dioramas or live hand-drawing (which don't).
- **It is inspired, not copied.** It deeply borrows the *grammar and soul* that worked for
  the studied creators — **@itsemilyhiggins** (tactile-tabletop translation of abstract
  ideas) and **@dr.patricio.ochoa** (the trust-sandwich chassis, anthropomorphizing the
  abstract, the hero-object bookend) — and adapts the **device**, never the props. See
  their teardowns in `Creator Analysis/<handle>/broll-dna.md`.
- **It is v1 and aspirational.** Jaime has little of his own posted work to extract from
  yet, so this is a deliberate remix calibrated to his brand. Revisit it after ~5–10 of
  his own reels and let his actual footage overwrite the borrowed parts.
- **How it relates to the other files:** `me.md` + `content-strategy.md` = the brand/why;
  this = the visual identity/what; `broll-style.md` = the craft/how (Fidelity Gate +
  Omni Flash prompt template). This file sets the *world and the moves*; `broll-style.md`
  tells you how to write a faithful prompt for each shot.

## 1. The Soul — what a Jaime reel should FEEL like

A Jaime reel feels like **a sharp, warm founder taking something you were told is
complicated and scary — AI inside your business — and quietly putting it in your hands as
something small, physical, and obvious.** The trust mechanism is **demystification through
the tangible**: the b-roll's only job is to take an invisible idea (an agent, a workflow,
data architecture, ROI) and make it a *real object you can see work*, so a non-technical
founder like Ricardo *gets it* without ever feeling stupid.

Three feelings the visuals must protect, straight out of `me.md` / `content-strategy.md`:

- **Not scary, high-end, friendly.** Warm, premium, human. The opposite of the cold,
  hypey "glowing-blue-AI-brain" aesthetic — that look *is the thing Jaime positions
  against* (vaporware, hype, "AI will replace your team"). Our world is warm wood and
  paper, not neon and chrome.
- **Partner, not vendor.** The visuals are generous and explanatory, never a slick product
  ad. We show the *concept* working, not a dashboard selling.
- **"I was you two years ago."** Grounded, real, a little handmade-feeling. Premium but
  not untouchable — the warmth is what says "this guy is on my side."

If a shot is beautiful but reads as *enterprise AI hype*, it has failed the soul check,
even if it's technically faithful. Faithful **and** warm-human-premium.

## 1B. The Running Anchor — the first question (choose per reel)

**Before any shot is designed, answer one question: _who is the Running Anchor, and why?_**
The Running Anchor is the reel's **main character** — the ONE evolving visual that recurs and
updates across the whole piece as a progress map (borrowed from Emily's "running anchor"). The
timeline, the cards, the curves, and the metaphors all *hang off it*. It is chosen **per
script, never defaulted** — a different reel wants a different anchor.

**The test it must pass — it has to do two jobs at once:**
- **TRACK** — carry the information the viewer must follow (years, names, steps, numbers) so
  the piece stays legible.
- **FEEL** — embody the emotional thesis of the script in its very shape/behavior (e.g.
  acceleration, accumulation, collapse, growth) so the point is *seen*, not just narrated.

**Method: criticize your first pick until it makes sense.** The first idea is usually a
*stage* (a flat timeline = only shows *when*) or a *metaphor* (a creature = hides the data).
Attack it: does it serve BOTH track and feel? If not, revise — keep going until one concept
does both. _Worked example (the LLM-timeline reel): "the flat timeline" failed FEEL (can't
show acceleration); "the AI as a creature" failed TRACK (hides the model names). The answer
was **"The Climb"** — an ascending timeline that steepens each year: the years + planted model
cards keep it legible (TRACK), the steepening slope IS the acceleration (FEEL)._

Once the anchor is chosen, **pick each beat's visual mode** off it: **illustrative** (a
labeled, in-world graphic — the default for explainer content), **metaphor** (an iconic object
— reserved for emotional beats), or **hybrid**. Metaphor is seasoning, not the spine.

## 2. The Concept World — "The Operator's Tabletop" (the ONE locked world)

**Every explanatory b-roll lives in one world: a warm, cinematic, macro tabletop where
the invisible machinery of a business is staged as real, tactile miniature objects.**
Jaime's topics — operations drowning in manual work, automation, data architecture, ROI,
agents — all get translated onto **a real wooden desk / workshop surface**: wood-block
warehouses and storefronts, tiny figurines of overworked admins, stacks of paper invoices,
filing trays, conveyor belts of tasks, coins and a balance scale for money/ROI, tangled
vs. clean cabling for data, a small sleek machine/robot token for "the AI." Shot **macro,
shallow depth of field**, one element razor-sharp, the rest melting to bokeh.

This is our adaptation of Emily's *tabletop translator* — but the props come from **the
founder's operational world**, and the grade is **ours**.

### The Look (lock these)
- **Palette — warm human base + ONE cool accent.** The whole world is warm: honey wood,
  kraft paper, cream card, brass, soft amber daylight from one side. The **single accent is
  cool** — a confident teal/electric-cyan — and it is reserved for **the AI/automation
  element only**. Semantics that are pure Jaime: *the warm world is the human business; the
  one cool-glowing thing is the AI entering it.* One accent, one meaning, every reel.
  _(Knob: if Ai-me/iBritha have brand colors, set the accent to the brand's cool/tech hue.)_
- **Light:** warm key from one side (window or practical lamp), soft, directional, with
  gentle raking shadows so the tactile texture reads. Premium, low-contrast, filmic — not
  flat, not clinical.
- **Materials = message.** Real, analog, slightly handmade: wood, paper, kraft, brass,
  ceramic, fabric. Tactile imperfection = trustworthy and human (and it's exactly what AI
  video renders convincingly). The cool AI token is the ONE sleek/modern object in an
  otherwise warm, analog world — its difference is the point.
- **Numbers and data live in-world — as props OR as tactile illustrative graphics.** ROI, %,
  pesos, counts, timelines, growth curves = stamped brass plates, coins, a chalk figure on a
  slate, a paper ledger, **or** a line carved/branded into the wood, a curve drawn on kraft,
  named cards planted in a row. The test is *in-world and tactile*, not *prop vs. graphic*:
  **no floating neon dashboards or holographic charts** — but a warm, carved, hand-made
  timeline or chart is not only allowed, it's often the clearest choice (see §7 and §1B).

### Why this world is *ours*
- It makes Jaime's exact thesis visible: *messy, human, manual operations* (warm, cluttered,
  tactile) vs. *one clean automated path* (the cool accent). The aesthetic argues the point
  before he says a word.
- It's the anti-hype look by construction — warm and handmade where the category is cold and
  slick — which is his entire positioning.
- It generates beautifully with AI video (real objects + real light + real motion).

## 3. The Face World — Jaime on camera

The talking-head is the trust anchor; it should feel like the **same world, same warmth** as
the tabletop so the reel reads as one cohesive piece (one temperature, like Patricio — not a
hard hot/cold split). Recommended default, to be locked to Jaime's real set:

- **Environment:** a warm, lived-in **founder's workspace** — wood desk, a shelf with a few
  real objects (books, a ceramic, a plant), soft daylight or a warm practical lamp glowing
  behind. Grounded and real, not a studio cyclorama.
- **Framing:** centered medium-close, eye-level, leaning slightly in. Hands free to gesture
  (Jaime talks with his hands — let them enter frame).
- **Authority prop (pick ONE and keep it every reel):** a visible podcast mic, or a signature
  recurring object, that makes every face shot unmistakably his.
- **Grade:** the same warm amber/daylight as the tabletop, so cutting between face and
  concept-world feels seamless.

them once and keep them constant — consistency of the face-world is half the recognizability.)_

## 4. Signature Devices — our owned moves

Each is a *replicable move*, adapted from the studied creators to Jaime's world and made
AI-generatable. For each: what it does · why it lands (soul) · when the script calls for it ·
how to keep/swap.

### The Running Anchor (the main character — pick it FIRST)
- **What:** ONE evolving visual that recurs across the whole reel and updates at each beat as a
  living progress map — the timeline that climbs, the system that assembles, the curve that
  rises. See §1B for how to choose it (it must serve TRACK + FEEL).
- **Why:** it gives the viewer one thing to follow and lets them *feel* the script's arc in a
  single object — the strongest retention + comprehension device we have. (From Emily's
  "running anchor.")
- **When:** every explainer/story reel. Choose it FIRST, before any individual shot.
- **Keep/Swap:** KEEP "one evolving visual, updated per beat, serving track + feel." SWAP the
  anchor per script — an ascending timeline for a history reel, an assembling system for a
  how-it-works reel, a rising/draining curve for an ROI reel.

### The Operator's Tabletop (marquee device)
- **What:** translate any invisible AI/business concept into a tactile miniature staged on the
  warm desk. The prop set IS the explanation.
- **Why:** makes the abstract concrete so a non-technical founder *sees* it — the core trust
  mechanism. (From Emily's tabletop translator + "make the abstract physical.")
- **When:** any explanatory beat — what an agent is, how a workflow runs, what "clean data"
  means, where ROI comes from.
- **Keep/Swap:** KEEP the warm macro tabletop, one-sharp-element shallow DOF, props-as-data.
  SWAP the specific objects per script — reinvent, never reuse the same toy twice.

### The Cool Intruder (accent rule = semantics)
- **What:** the warm world is entirely warm; **the AI/automation element is the single
  cool-glowing object** (a sleek token, a glowing teal line threading the scene, a small
  modern machine). Exactly one cool accent per shot, and it always marks "the AI."
- **Why:** the eye learns instantly "the cool thing = the AI," and it stages Jaime's whole
  story — *the calm machine entering the messy human business* — as a visual, not a claim.
  (Adapts Emily's one-accent rule, loaded with Jaime-specific meaning.)
- **When:** any shot where AI/automation acts on the business.
- **Keep/Swap:** KEEP "warm everywhere, one cool accent = the AI." SWAP the object the accent
  rides.

### Drowning → One Clean Path (the before/after)
- **What:** fill the frame with many identical units to show **operations drowning** (a heap
  of paper invoices, a crowd of overwhelmed little figurines, a tangle of wires, a phone
  buzzing with 100 messages), then resolve to **ONE clean automated path** (a single cool line
  routing it all, one calm conveyor, a tidy stack).
- **Why:** it's literally Ricardo's pain (drowning admin) and Jaime's thesis (clean systems)
  rendered as one image — emotional, not numerical. (Emily's hero-amid-the-mass + Patricio's
  villain/hero + Jaime's before/after.)
- **When:** the pain beat, the "here's what most founders live with," the transformation.
- **Keep/Swap:** KEEP mass-of-identical-units → one clean exception/path. SWAP the units.

### Money on the Table (the ROI / finance lens — his under-used edge)
- **What:** render cost, payback, ROI as **physical money objects**: coins stacking or
  draining, a balance scale weighing "hire vs. automate," a paper ledger, a brass plate
  stamped with the number. The finance pillar nobody else in his lane covers, made visual.
- **Why:** Jaime's accounting background is a differentiator; showing ROI as tangible money a
  founder can feel is on-brand and rare. (Emily's "numbers are objects.")
- **When:** any ROI / cost / payback / "is it worth it" beat (Pillar 4).
- **Keep/Swap:** KEEP money/measure as real props + diegetic numbers. SWAP coins↔bills↔scale↔
  ledger per script.

### The Honest Build (watch it get made)
- **What:** ONE continuous shot where a hand assembles the system in-frame — cards laid into a
  workflow, blocks stacked into a foundation, a cool wire plugged into the warm machine, a
  messy desk tidied into one clean line. The build is the explanation.
- **Why:** "watch a real person build it" reads as honest labor, not a polished asset — the
  exact "I learned this from zero, I'll show you the real thing" energy. (Patricio's growing
  drawing + Emily's one-prop-per-noun, kept short/keyframed so AI can generate it.)
- **When:** "here's how the pieces fit," foundations, anatomy-of-a-system beats.
- **Keep/Swap:** KEEP one continuous in-frame build that accretes. SWAP the medium (cards,
  blocks, plugging-in, tidying). Keep builds short (one motion idea) so AI generation holds.

### Trust Sandwich (the structural chassis)
- **What:** face HOOK (~10%) → tabletop BODY (the explanation) → face PAYOFF/turn (~10%).
  Return to Jaime's face right when the b-roll would start to tire.
- **Why:** human warmth brackets the explanation; the face re-injects energy and
  re-personalizes the lesson. (Patricio's default chassis.)
- **When:** essentially every teaching/story reel 60s+.
- **Keep/Swap:** KEEP the ~10/80/10 rhythm and the return-to-face for the resolution.

### Hero-Object Bookend
- **What:** open and close on the ONE object that embodies the topic (the phone showing the
  app, the cool AI token, the finished clean workflow). Reserve the single biggest camera move
  — a slow macro push-to-lens — for that closing reveal.
- **Why:** one object anchors the whole reel and creates a visual rhyme = felt closure without
  an outro card. (Patricio's hero-object bookend.)
- **When:** any reel with a physical or drawable centerpiece.
- **Keep/Swap:** KEEP "present it at the open, rhyme it at the close, save your one big move
  for the reveal." SWAP the object.

## 5. Motion Signature

- **Camera: locked-but-breathing.** No static tripod, no fast whips. Every b-roll gets a slow,
  weighty move — a 5–8% push-in, drift, glide, or gentle orbit over 3–5s — with foreground
  parallax so it reads as a real macro shot, not a fake zoom. Slow + heavy = premium.
- **Hero-locked / world-moves** on the key beat: hold the frame and move ONE object through it
  (a cool line routing the chaos, a coin dropping onto the scale). The object's motion is the
  story beat.
- **One reserved big move.** Save the single largest push / macro reveal for the climax or the
  hero-object bookend close — never blow it early.
- **Cut on the keyword,** voice-locked. New noun/idea = new shot; the hard cut lands on the
  emphasized word, in sync with the captions.
- **One motion idea per clip.** Premium, not tacky — no flare spam, no five things happening at
  once. (This also keeps AI generation from morphing.)

## 6. Caption Identity

- **Word-by-word / short 2–4 word kinetic captions,** dead-center or nested into the scene,
  hard-synced to the voiceover — the caption build *is* the cut metronome (carries rhythm even
  over a single long shot).
- **Two registers:** bold sans for normal/structure; a reserved style — the **cool accent
  color** or a heavier weight — flags the key term / number / punch word. Mirrors "the cool
  thing is the point" rule from the visuals.
- **Language: Mexican Spanish** by default (IG), matching the spoken track. Keep terms short so
  they render cleanly.

## 7. What We DON'T Do (visual anti-patterns)

The visual equivalent of `anti-patterns.md`. These are auto-rejects — most of them *because*
they read as the hypey, scary AI aesthetic Jaime positions against:

- **No glowing-blue-AI-brain / neon-chrome / sci-fi hologram look.** That's the vaporware
  aesthetic. Our AI is a small calm cool token in a warm human world, not a Tron grid.
- **No floating neon UI, dashboards, holographic charts, or code rain.** But illustrative
  graphics rendered *in-world* — a timeline carved into the wood, a curve drawn on kraft, a
  growth line of stacked blocks, named cards dealt in a row — are **encouraged when they teach
  better than a metaphor.** Choose per beat: default **illustrative** for explainer/timeline
  content, reserve **metaphor** for emotional/iconic beats. The ban is on *cold flat UI*, not
  on charts as such.
- **No corporate stock clichés** — robot-shaking-hands, humanoid android at a desk, a brain
  made of circuits, a businessman touching a holographic screen. Instant trust-killers.
- **No cold, desaturated, clinical grade.** We are warm, always. Cold appears only as the
  single accent.
- **No flat motion-graphics / slide-deck animation.** Everything lives in one tactile world.
- **No literal on-the-nose icons** (a red "ERROR" popup, a lightbulb for "idea", a padlock for
  "security"). Faithful AND evocative — pass the Fidelity Gate in `broll-style.md` §1b.
- **No decorative-but-wrong shots** — a pretty object loosely tied to a noun (an insect for a
  software "bug"). The metaphor must decode to the *exact* idea.
- **No reusing the same prop reel-to-reel.** Reinvent the objects each time; the *grammar* is
  the constant, not the toys.
- **No two-temperature clash.** Face-world and concept-world share one warm grade (we chose a
  single locked world).

## 8. How this feeds the b-roll director

When directing a reel's b-roll (`reel-broll-director`), the order is:

0. **Choose the Running Anchor FIRST (see §1B)** — name it and criticize the first pick until
   it serves both what the viewer must TRACK and what they must FEEL. Everything below hangs
   off it.
1. **This file** — it sets the *world, palette, devices, and anti-patterns*. Every shot
   is staged in The Operator's Tabletop, warm + one cool accent, using one of the Signature
   Devices.
2. **Then `broll-style.md`** — it provides the *craft*: the Fidelity Gate (does the metaphor
   decode to the exact idea?), the Shared DNA, and the Omni Flash prompt template (how to write
   the rich, copy-paste, generation-ready paragraph).

So the loop is: **Jaime's script → this identity picks the world + device for each beat →
`broll-style.md` turns each into a faithful, on-palette Omni Flash prompt.** The result is
b-roll that is both *correct* (Fidelity Gate) and *unmistakably his* (this file).

**Topic → world quick map** (examples, reinvent props per script):
- *"What an AI agent actually is"* → The Honest Build: a hand assembling a little worker-token
  from parts; the cool accent is its core.
- *"Your operations are drowning"* → Drowning → One Clean Path: a heap of invoices/figurines,
  resolved by one cool routing line.
- *"Clean your data before you automate"* → tangled warm wires vs. one clean labeled set; the
  cool accent only connects once it's tidy.
- *"Hire vs. automate (ROI)"* → Money on the Table: a brass balance scale, coins vs. a single
  cool machine token.
- *"ChatGPT isn't a strategy"* → a lone warm gadget on a desk vs. a built system of connected
  blocks — tool vs. system, made physical.
